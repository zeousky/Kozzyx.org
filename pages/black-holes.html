black holes, briefly

what happens when gravity stops being polite

A black hole is a region of spacetime where gravity is so strong that, beyond a boundary called the event horizon, nothing can escape to the outside universe. Not light, not matter, not the excellent plan you had five minutes ago.

how black holes form

Many stellar-mass black holes form when massive stars exhaust their fuel and their cores collapse. If enough mass is compressed into a small enough region, gravity overwhelms the mechanisms that normally resist collapse.

There are also supermassive black holes in the centers of galaxies. Their origins are still an active research topic, but they can contain millions or billions of times the mass of the Sun.

the event horizon

The event horizon is not a physical surface like the crust of a planet. It is a boundary in spacetime. Once something crosses it, every possible future path leads farther inward.

To a distant observer, objects falling toward the horizon appear increasingly slowed and redshifted. From the falling object's own perspective, crossing a sufficiently large horizon would not necessarily involve a dramatic local event.

what is at the center

Classical general relativity predicts a singularity, where density and curvature become mathematically extreme. Most physicists take that as a sign that our current theories are incomplete under those conditions.

A quantum theory of gravity would likely be needed to describe the deepest interior correctly. Conveniently, nature placed the answer behind an event horizon.

how we detect them

Black holes themselves do not emit ordinary light, but matter around them can become extremely hot and bright as it falls inward.

Astronomers also infer black holes from the motion of nearby stars, gravitational waves from black-hole mergers, and radio observations of material near event horizons.

small facts
  • Black holes can spin.
  • Merging black holes produce gravitational waves.
  • The event horizon size increases with mass.
  • Supermassive black holes are found in the centers of many large galaxies.

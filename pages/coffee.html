coffee is a very elaborate bean drink

botany, roasting, chemistry, culture, and millions of people pretending 7 a.m. is survivable

Coffee looks simple only after an absurd supply chain has finished doing complicated things to it. A coffee cherry grows on a plant, gets processed, dried, shipped, roasted, ground, extracted with water, and then consumed by someone who says they can definitely taste stone fruit.

coffee starts as fruit

Coffee beans are seeds found inside the fruit of coffee plants. The fruit is commonly called a coffee cherry because of its shape and color when ripe.

The two commercially dominant species are Coffea arabica and Coffea canephora, the latter commonly called robusta.

processing changes flavor

After harvesting, producers need to remove or dry the fruit around the seeds. Common approaches include washed, natural, and honey-style processing.

These methods influence fermentation, drying, sweetness, acidity, and the final flavor profile. The farm did a lot of work before your grinder got involved.

roasting

Green coffee seeds are relatively dense and smell nothing like brewed coffee. Roasting drives chemical reactions that create familiar aromas and flavors.

Lighter roasts tend to preserve more origin character and acidity. Darker roasting pushes flavor toward caramelized, smoky, bitter, and roasted notes.

extraction

Brewing is controlled dissolving. Water removes soluble compounds from ground coffee. Grind size, temperature, contact time, water chemistry, and the coffee-to-water ratio all affect the result.

Too little extraction can taste sharp or sour. Too much can become dry and bitter. Humans responded to this by buying scales accurate to tenths of a gram.

small facts
  • Coffee beans are seeds, not botanical beans.
  • Arabica generally grows best at higher elevations than robusta.
  • Grinding increases surface area and speeds extraction.
  • Espresso is a brewing method, not a specific roast level.

cassette tapes

tiny plastic rectangles that made music portable and rewinding everyone’s problem

The compact cassette turned recorded audio into something cheap, portable, recordable, and easy to share. By modern standards it was inconvenient in almost every measurable way. Naturally, people loved it.

a format built for ordinary people

Cassettes were introduced in the 1960s and became enormously popular over the following decades. Unlike vinyl records, they were small and relatively durable. Unlike many earlier tape formats, they were easy to handle.

Most importantly, ordinary listeners could record onto them. Radio broadcasts, albums, voice notes, rehearsals, and homemade compilations all fit into the same little shell.

the mixtape

The cassette made personal sequencing easy. A mixtape could be a gift, a confession, a party soundtrack, an argument, or an extremely labor-intensive attempt to make someone think you had excellent taste.

Recording happened in real time. Choosing a song meant committing physical tape and minutes of your life. The limitation made curation feel deliberate.

why the sound was imperfect

Cassette tape stores sound magnetically. The narrow tape moves past a playback head, and tiny variations in magnetism become an electrical signal.

Tape hiss, wow and flutter, head alignment, wear, and cheap duplication could all reduce fidelity. Noise-reduction systems helped, but perfection was never really the format’s thing.

why they came back

Cassettes returned as a niche format because they are tangible, inexpensive to manufacture in small runs, and pleasantly inconvenient. A tape gives music an object, a sequence, and a beginning and end.

Nobody needs cassettes now. That is partly why they are fun.

small facts
  • A C60 cassette generally holds about 30 minutes per side.
  • The tape is wound between two small reels inside the shell.
  • Portable cassette players helped make private, mobile listening normal.
  • Mixtapes were one of the first mainstream forms of user-created music curation.

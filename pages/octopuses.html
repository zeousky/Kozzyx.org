octopuses are ridiculous

eight arms, three hearts, zero respect for normal animal design

Octopuses feel less like an ordinary animal and more like somebody submitted a creature concept after ignoring the assignment. They are soft-bodied, extremely curious, capable of solving problems, and built around a nervous system that spreads a surprising amount of processing into their arms.

the basic problem with octopuses

An octopus has no bones, which means it can squeeze through gaps barely larger than its beak. The beak is the hard limit. Everything else is basically clever biological luggage.

Their arms are covered in suckers that can grip, taste, and explore at the same time. Each arm can perform complex movements with a degree of independence, while the central brain coordinates the overall plan.

three hearts, because apparently one was too ordinary

Two hearts pump blood through the gills. A third sends oxygenated blood through the rest of the body. Their blood uses hemocyanin, a copper-based molecule, which works well in cold and low-oxygen conditions.

The system is effective, but expensive. When an octopus swims quickly, the body-wide heart can temporarily stop beating, which is one reason many species prefer crawling along the seafloor.

camouflage as performance art

Octopuses can alter both color and skin texture. Specialized pigment cells called chromatophores change visible color, while other structures help reflect light and create patterns.

Some species can raise little bumps and ridges across their skin, making themselves resemble rock, coral, or algae. It is less 'turn invisible' and more 'become suspiciously convincing scenery.'

the intelligence question

Octopuses can open containers, navigate mazes, distinguish shapes, and learn from experience. In aquariums, they are notorious for investigating objects that were absolutely not placed there for them.

Their intelligence evolved separately from vertebrate intelligence, which makes them especially interesting. Nature arrived at complex problem-solving more than once, using very different hardware.

short version

Octopuses are weird because their bodies and minds solve familiar problems in unfamiliar ways. They are a reminder that evolution is not aiming for one correct design. It is throwing ideas at physics until something survives long enough to reproduce.

small facts
  • Most octopus species are solitary.
  • Many species have very short lifespans, often only a few years.
  • Their suckers contain sensory receptors that help them chemically sample surfaces.
  • An octopus can regenerate a lost arm.
